# Microsoft Valuation Model

An independently created three-statement financial model, DCF valuation and investment thesis for Microsoft Corporation (NASDAQ: MSFT).

**Valuation date:** 28 July 2026

## Project Overview

This project was created to develop my practical financial-modelling and fundamental equity-research skills.

I analysed Microsoft’s historical financial statements, forecast its financial performance from FY2026E to FY2030E and estimated an implied share price using a discounted cash flow valuation.

The project consists of an integrated Excel valuation model and a four-page investment thesis covering Microsoft’s investment case, valuation, catalysts and risks.

## Model Structure

The Excel workbook includes:

- Assumptions
- Income statement
- Balance sheet
- Cash flow statement
- Supporting schedules
- DCF valuation
- Sensitivity analysis

Historical financial information covers FY2023A to FY2025A, followed by a five-year forecast from FY2026E to FY2030E.

## Valuation Methodology

I valued Microsoft using a five-year unlevered free cash flow DCF and a Gordon Growth terminal value.

Unlevered free cash flow was calculated using:

> UFCF = EBIT × (1 − Tax Rate) + Depreciation and Amortisation − Capital Expenditure − Change in Net Working Capital

The present value of forecast UFCF and terminal value were combined to calculate enterprise value. Cash and short-term investments were then added and debt was deducted to calculate equity value.

Equity value was divided by diluted shares outstanding to calculate the implied share price.

## Investment Case

The principal factors supporting Microsoft’s investment case are:

- An integrated ecosystem across Microsoft 365, Azure, Windows, GitHub, security and gaming
- Strong enterprise relationships and recurring revenue
- Continued growth opportunities in Azure and cloud computing
- Potential monetisation of Microsoft 365 Copilot, GitHub Copilot and Azure AI
- Opportunities to increase revenue per customer through additional AI features

## Key Risks

The principal risks identified in my analysis are:

- AI monetisation failing to justify infrastructure investment
- Capital expenditure remaining elevated without comparable free-cash-flow growth
- Increasing competition across cloud computing, AI and developer tools
- Slower growth across Microsoft’s more mature products
- Consolidated forecasts understating the growth potential of individual business areas

## Sensitivity Analysis

The sensitivity analysis tests Microsoft’s implied share price using:

- WACC assumptions between 8.81% and 10.81%
- Terminal growth assumptions between 1.50% and 3.50%

The base case uses a WACC of 9.81% and a terminal growth rate of 2.50%, producing an implied share price of $239.07.
